### 1. Budget 2026 — SME automation & digitalisation funding

- **Update:** Budget 2026 (tabled Oct 2025) concentrates support on SME automation,
  digitalisation and AI adoption. Repeatedly cited instruments:
  - **MSME Digital Grant MADANI** — reported ~RM50m allocation, 50% matching grant up to
    RM5,000 for approved digital tools, targeting up to ~100,000 SMEs.
  - **Digital Acceleration Grant** — reported ~RM53m for AI, blockchain and cybersecurity
    adoption.
  - **TECHAD Fund** — reported ~RM300m, up to ~RM10m per company for automation,
    OMS/WMS upgrades and AI integration.
  - **Bank Negara Malaysia** — reported ~RM3.8b facility for SME automation,
    digitalisation and sustainability.
  - **SJPP loan guarantees** — reported ~RM30b, up to 80% guarantee for SMEs including
    manufacturing.
  - **Outcome-Based Incentive Framework** for SMEs — targeted tax incentives tied to
    adopting automation, digitalisation and sustainability.
  - **50% tax deduction** for certified AI and cybersecurity training.
- **Effective date:** 2026 year of assessment / programme year; individual scheme windows
  vary and open through 2026.
- **Businesses affected:** Malaysian SMEs and mid-tier manufacturers.
- **Possible marketing impact:** Funding availability is a credible, time-bound
  buying-trigger angle for MES/shop-floor digitalisation content. A plain-language
  "which grant fits an MES project" explainer is a likely content gap (competitor social
  activity is unaudited, but vendor education on this is thin). **Do not** state specific
  grant amounts, coverage percentages or eligibility as fact until confirmed against the
  administering agency — quote them as "reported" and link the primary scheme page.
- **Evidence type:** INDUSTRY SOURCE (secondary).
- **Confidence:** LOW-MEDIUM.
- **Contradicting evidence:** Allocation figures differ between sources; administering
  agencies (BSN / BPMB / MDEC / BSN-appointed) are stated inconsistently.
- **Evidence gaps:** No primary MOF Budget 2026 speech / appendix read; no confirmation
  schemes are open, unchanged, or not yet exhausted as of Sept 2026.

### 2. e-Invoice (LHDN MyInvois) — Phase 4 and threshold change

- **Update:**
  - **Phase 4** covers businesses with annual turnover **RM1m–RM5m**, mandatory from
    **1 January 2026**, with a **relaxation period extended to 31 December 2027**.
    During relaxation, consolidated e-invoices are permitted **except** individual
    transactions above **RM10,000**, which must be issued individually.
  - **Exemption threshold raised from RM500,000 to RM1,000,000** turnover, effective
    **1 January 2026** — businesses below RM1m turnover are exempt.
  - **Phase 5 was cancelled** following a Prime Minister announcement on **7 December 2025**.
- **Effective date:** 1 January 2026 (Phase 4 start); relaxation to 31 December 2027.
- **Businesses affected:** Smaller Malaysian manufacturers in the RM1m–RM5m band — a
  segment overlapping Mysoft's SME target. Sub-RM1m firms now out of scope.
- **Possible marketing impact:** e-Invoice compliance is pushing small manufacturers to
  adopt structured digital records and (often) accounting/ERP integration for the first
  time. This normalises "digital record of what happened" thinking — an adjacency to
  production data capture and the digital Job Traveller narrative. Possible content
  angle: "your invoices are now digital and traceable — your shop floor isn't."
  Keep this as adjacency, not a compliance claim; Mysoft MES is not an e-Invoice solution.
- **Evidence type:** INDUSTRY SOURCE (secondary, tax-advisory).
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM (phase structure is widely and consistently reported);
  LOW-MEDIUM on enforcement/penalty dates.
- **Contradicting evidence:** Sources differ on full-enforcement/penalty start for
  Phase 4 — variously "1 January 2027" (penalties) and "1 January 2028" (full
  enforcement).
- **Evidence gaps:** No direct read of the current LHDN e-Invoice Guideline / SOP.
- **Sources:** ClearTax MY — e-Invoice phases & relaxation
  (https://www.cleartax.com/my/en/different-phases-implementation-timelines-einvoicing-malaysia);
  JomeInvoice — LHDN e-Invoice Malaysia 2026 guide

### 3. Foreign-worker policy — declining cap + Multi-Tier Levy Model

- **Update:** Under the 13th Malaysia Plan, the government is lowering the foreign-worker
  ceiling to **~13% of the workforce in 2026** (from ~15%), targeting **10% by 2030**,
  and is explicitly urging businesses to automate. The **Multi-Tier Levy Model (MTLM)** —
  a levy that scales with a company's reliance on foreign labour, replacing the flat
  sector rate — is reported as confirmed for **2026** implementation (previously slated
  for 2025). Current manufacturing levy cited at **RM1,850/year** (Peninsular Malaysia).
- **Effective date:** Cap reductions phased 2026–2030; MTLM reported for 2026.
- **Businesses affected:** Labour-intensive Malaysian manufacturers, including discrete
  manufacturers in Mysoft's target industries.
- **Possible marketing impact:** This is a structural push toward automation and
  higher labour productivity. It strengthens the "do more with the same headcount /
  reduce manual reporting effort" angle for production managers and GMs. Frame as
  operational efficiency and data-capture-without-extra-clerks, **not** as headcount
  reduction claims (unsupported and off-tone).
- **Evidence type:** PRIMARY EXTERNAL (policy direction, via news) + INDUSTRY SOURCE.
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM on direction; LOW-MEDIUM on exact MTLM timing and rates.
- **Contradicting evidence:** MTLM start date has slipped before; 2026 not fully
  confirmed against a gazetted instrument here.
- **Evidence gaps:** No primary MOHR / gazette text read; MTLM tier bands unknown.
- **Sources:** The Edge Malaysia — 10% foreign-worker cap by 2030, automation push
  (https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/815220);
  Human Resources Online — 10% cap by 2030
  (https://www.humanresourcesonline.net/malaysia-targets-10-cap-on-foreign-workforce-by-2030-as-government-pushes-businesses-to-invest-in-automation);
  Carriera — Foreign Worker Levy Malaysia 2026

### 4. NIMP 2030 — mid-term review due end-2026

- **Update:** The New Industrial Master Plan 2030 completes **Phase 1 (2023–2026)** this
  year; a **mid-term review is scheduled by end-2026** to reassess targets and progress.
  Phase 2 (2027–2030) shifts to implementation at scale. Smart-factory ambition:
  **upgrade 3,000 factories** to smart factories (~RM5b investment). NIMP 2030 Strategic
  Co-Investment Fund had backed **40 SMEs/mid-tier companies with ~RM75m** as at
  30 June 2026 (E&E, chemicals, pharma, food processing, ICT).
- **Effective date:** Ongoing; mid-term review end-2026.
- **Businesses affected:** Malaysian manufacturers, especially E&E and priority sectors.
- **Possible marketing impact:** "Smart factory" remains a funded national priority —
  useful authority/context framing. Watch the mid-term review for changed targets or
  new instruments that could become content triggers.
- **Evidence type:** PRIMARY EXTERNAL (MIDA) + INDUSTRY SOURCE.
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM.
- **Contradicting evidence:** None material found.
- **Evidence gaps:** Fund detail and smart-factory progress numbers not independently
  verified.
- **Sources:** MIDA — Launch of NIMP 2030
  (https://www.mida.gov.my/launch-of-the-new-industrial-master-plan-2030-nimp-2030/);
  The Star — "NIMP 2030 impetus for factory gate activity in 1Q"
