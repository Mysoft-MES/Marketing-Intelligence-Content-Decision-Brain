### 1. Malaysian manufacturing growth is positive but subdued (PMI)

- **Development:** S&P Global Malaysia Manufacturing PMI was **50.2 in August 2026**,
  down from 50.7 in June and July — a third straight month above the neutral 50 line,
  but the weakest of that run. New orders rose only marginally; production moderated;
  employment rose for the first time in four months; input and output price inflation
  eased to six-month lows. S&P Global notes the historical PMI–GDP relationship points
  to improving output growth in Q3 2026.
- **Geography:** Malaysia (national).
- **Relevance to Mysoft:** Demand environment for manufacturing capex is cautiously
  positive, not booming. Buyers will scrutinise payback and implementation effort.
- **Possible marketing impact:** Favour ROI-aware, low-disruption framing
  (fast to value, works alongside existing ERP/paper during rollout). Avoid
  boom-time "everyone is investing" language.
- **2026-09-03 addendum:** A complementary capacity signal — **manufacturing capacity
  utilisation rose to 83.7% in Q2 2026, up 1.2 percentage points year-on-year** (DOSM,
  reported via The Star, Aug 2026). Consistent with the PMI picture: steady utilisation,
  not a surge. Reinforces the "buyers scrutinise payback" read rather than changing it.
- **Evidence type:** PRIMARY EXTERNAL (S&P Global; DOSM) via news.
- **Confidence:** HIGH on the data points; MEDIUM on forward read.
- **Contradicting evidence:** None — sources agree on 50.2.
- **Evidence gaps:** No sub-sector (E&E vs machinery vs plastics) PMI breakdown.
- **Sources:** New Straits Times — "Malaysia's manufacturing sector posts slight
  improvement in August: PMI data"
  (https://www.nst.com.my/business/corporate/2026/09/1523148/malaysias-manufacturing-sector-posts-slight-improvement-august);
  The Edge Malaysia — "Malaysia's manufacturing PMI moderates in August"
  (https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/816342);
  S&P Global PMI press release
  (https://www.pmi.spglobal.com/Public/Home/PressRelease/7171bf5c418d4a9894acc61ad4b52aa5).
- **Last checked:** 2026-09-02.

### 2. NIMP 2030 smart-factory agenda advancing

- **Development:** Manufacturing gross value-added rose **5.9% year-on-year in Q1 2026**,
  attributed in part to NIMP 2030 momentum. Government funding vehicles (Strategic
  Co-Investment Fund: ~40 companies, ~RM75m by mid-2026) and a stated target of
  **3,000 smart-factory upgrades (~RM5b)** keep manufacturing digitalisation a
  national priority. A **mid-term review is due by end-2026**.
- **Geography:** Malaysia.
- **Relevance to Mysoft:** "Smart factory / data-driven production" has sustained
  policy air cover — helpful for authority-led content and for prospects seeking
  internal justification.
- **Possible marketing impact:** Cite the national direction as context (not as a
  Mysoft claim). Monitor the end-2026 review for new grants or targets.
- **Evidence type:** PRIMARY EXTERNAL (MIDA, DOSM via news) + INDUSTRY SOURCE.
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM.
- **Contradicting evidence:** Commentary (Fulcrum) flags implementation risk / slow
  execution against ambitious targets.
- **Evidence gaps:** No verified count of factories actually upgraded to date.
- **Sources:** The Star — NIMP 2030 factory-gate activity
  (https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2026/07/24/nimp-2030-impetus-for-factory-gate-activity-in-1q);
  Fulcrum — "Malaysia's New Industrial Master Plan: Clear-Eyed on Implementation"
  (https://fulcrum.sg/malaysias-new-industrial-master-plan-clear-eyed-on-implementation/).
- **Last checked:** 2026-09-02.

### 3. Penang E&E investment concentration continues

- **Development:** Penang secured **~RM4.9b in approved manufacturing investment in
  Q1 2026** (second nationwide), ~70% foreign. E&E plus machinery & equipment made up
  ~RM3.6b (74%). Penang recorded **>RM350b in E&E exports in H1 2026** and routinely
  accounts for >60% of national E&E exports. Recent facility investments: Aixtron
  (Germany, ~EUR40m, 2026–2027), Chipbond (Taiwan, advanced packaging & test, ~USD200m,
  opened Feb 2026), MKS semiconductor equipment supercentre (phase 1, May 2026).
  Penang's blueprint targets a move up the value chain toward chip design.
- **Geography:** Penang / Northern Malaysia — Mysoft's home region.
- **Relevance to Mysoft:** The local target market is E&E / semiconductor supply chain
  heavy. These buyers face rising customer traceability, audit and data-integrity
  requirements — core to the MES value story. Also a growing pool of new/expanding
  plants standing up production systems.
- **Possible marketing impact:** Regionally-specific content ("Penang precision
  manufacturers", supplier-to-semiconductor traceability) is well justified. Supports
  a LinkedIn + Facebook Northern-Malaysia geo-targeting angle.
- **Evidence type:** PRIMARY EXTERNAL (InvestPenang / MIDA via news).
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH on investment figures; MEDIUM on interpretation.
- **Contradicting evidence:** None material; talent-shortage coverage is a
  countervailing constraint on the region's growth.
- **Evidence gaps:** No data on how many of these plants are MES-ready or in Mysoft's
  size band.
- **Sources:** The Edge Malaysia — "Penang secures RM4.9 bil in approved manufacturing
  investments in 1Q" (https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/807633);
  InvestPenang — Electrical & Electronics (https://investpenang.gov.my/electrical-electronics/);
  Entrepreneur APAC — "After Building a Chip Manufacturing Hub, Penang Wants a Bigger
  Slice of the Value Chain"
  (https://apac.entrepreneur.com/business-news/after-building-a-chip-manufacturing-hub-penang-wants-a-bigger-slice-of-the-value-chain).

### 4. US tariffs: direct hit to Malaysian E&E/semis limited so far

- **Development:** Reporting indicates semiconductors carry a ~99.9% exemption rate from
  new US tariffs, E&E products ~67%, and that only ~4.2% of Malaysia's total exports
  face the new US tariffs. Malaysian exports to the US were up ~54.9% in H1 2026.
  Industry executives expect limited immediate impact; the identified risk is
  **indirect** — weaker global demand feeding through to lower regional orders.
- **Geography:** Malaysia; US trade exposure.
- **Relevance to Mysoft:** Reduces near-term "customers frozen by tariffs" risk to the
  pipeline, but demand softness remains a scenario. Efficiency/cost-control messaging
  hedges either way.
- **Possible marketing impact:** Don't build campaigns around tariff fear; if
  referenced at all, frame as "resilience through better operational control".
- **Evidence type:** INDUSTRY SOURCE (analyst commentary via news).
- **Confidence:** LOW-MEDIUM (fast-moving trade policy).
- **Contradicting evidence:** Some coverage stresses downside risk from a broader
  trade slowdown; exemption percentages vary by source and date.
- **Evidence gaps:** Tariff terms change frequently — revalidate before any use.
- **Sources:** The Edge Malaysia — "Only 4.2% of Malaysia's exports face new US
  tariffs as key tech sector spared — Phillip Capital"
  (https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/813559);
  Digitimes — "US semiconductor tariffs may weigh on Malaysia's export-driven
  industries" (https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20260120VL206/malaysia-chips-manufacturing-usa-tariffs-policy.html).
- **Last checked:** 2026-09-02.

### 5. MES category: growing market, shift to modular/cloud

- **Development:** Third-party estimates put the **global MES market at ~USD 17–19b in
  2026**, growing ~9–12% annually toward ~USD 29–42b in the early 2030s. Commentary
  describes a shift from rigid monolithic on-premise MES toward **modular, cloud-connected
  platforms**. Separately, Malaysia's smart-manufacturing / Industry 4.0 market is
  estimated at ~USD 5b, with **SME digital adoption still ~20%** and most manufacturing
  firms below 50% automation; Industry4WRD-linked matching grants of up to ~70% are
  cited for qualifying digitalisation (including MES).
- **Geography:** Global + Malaysia.
- **Relevance to Mysoft:** Confirms a large, underpenetrated local opportunity and a
  category trend (modular/cloud, incremental adoption) that suits a focused
  shop-floor-first product versus heavyweight suites.
- **Possible marketing impact:** Positioning angle — "start with visibility and the
  digital Job Traveller, expand later" fits the modular-adoption trend and the low
  current adoption base. Use market-size figures only as loose context, attributed.
- **2026-09-04 addendum:** Re-checked. Another aggregator puts the **global MES market at
  ~USD 20.58b in 2026** (from ~USD 19.09b in 2025), ~13.3% CAGR to ~USD 44.67b by 2034 —
  a slightly higher point estimate, same growth story. The SME-relevant theme is
  re-confirmed and sharper: modern MES described as **modular, cloud-based, fast to deploy,
  designed for phased adoption — "start small with basic performance tracking on a single
  line and scale only once value is proven."** This is a direct external tailwind for
  Mysoft's "start with the Digital Job Order, expand later" framing. No directional change;
  freshness only. Sources (accessed 2026-09-04): GlobeNewswire — "MES Market Expected to
  Reach US$44.67 Billion at 13.3% CAGR by 2034" (2026-06-29); ITI Group — "Why MES should

  Global Growth Insights — MES companies 2026 (USD 20.58b figure).
- **Evidence type:** INDUSTRY SOURCE (vendor/analyst blogs) — weak.
