# MARKETING INTELLIGENCE + CONTENT DECISION BRAIN
## Decision Framework

Version: 1.0
Last Updated: 2026-08-29

---

# 1. PURPOSE

This framework defines HOW the Marketing Intelligence + Content Decision Brain makes marketing decisions.

The Brain must not jump directly from:

INPUT
→ CONTENT IDEA

It must reason through:

BUSINESS OBJECTIVE
→ AUDIENCE
→ MARKET
→ COMPETITORS
→ PLATFORM
→ HISTORICAL PERFORMANCE
→ OPPORTUNITY
→ STRATEGIC CHOICE
→ CREATIVE DECISION
→ MEASUREMENT
→ LEARNING

The objective is to make recommendations that are:

- evidence-informed
- audience-specific
- platform-specific
- commercially relevant
- differentiated
- measurable
- capable of outperforming existing approaches

---

# 2. DECISION STARTING POINT

Every decision begins by defining the problem.

Determine:

BUSINESS:
What business or brand is involved?

PRODUCT:
What product or service is involved?

BUSINESS OBJECTIVE:
What are we trying to achieve?

MARKETING OBJECTIVE:
What marketing result supports that business objective?

AUDIENCE:
Who needs to take action?

FUNNEL STAGE:
Where are they in the buying journey?

TIMEFRAME:
Is there urgency?

CONSTRAINTS:
Budget, production capability, manpower, platform, timing or other restrictions.

Do not recommend creative before understanding the objective.

Possible objectives include:

## ENGAGEMENT

- comments
- shares

## EDUCATION

Primary metrics may include:

- watch time
- completion
- saves

## TRAFFIC

- clicks

## LEAD GENERATION

- enquiries
- qualified leads
- CPL
- demo requests

## CONVERSION

# 4. UNDERSTAND THE AUDIENCE

Retrieve the relevant audience intelligence.

Determine:

WHO ARE THEY?

WHAT ARE THEY RESPONSIBLE FOR?

WHAT DO THEY WANT?

WHAT PROBLEM ARE THEY EXPERIENCING?

WHAT FRUSTRATES THEM?

WHAT ARE THEY AFRAID OF?

WHAT WOULD MAKE THEIR JOB OR LIFE EASIER?

WHAT DO THEY ALREADY KNOW?

WHAT DO THEY NOT UNDERSTAND?

WHAT DO THEY OBJECT TO?

WHAT WOULD MAKE THEM TRUST US?

WHAT WOULD CAUSE THEM TO TAKE ACTION?

---

# 5. IDENTIFY THE REAL PROBLEM

Do not confuse product features with customer problems.

Example:

FEATURE:
Real-time production dashboard.

CUSTOMER PROBLEM:
Management cannot quickly determine whether production is on schedule.

CONTENT should normally begin from the customer problem rather than the feature.

Translate:

FEATURE
→ FUNCTION
→ BUSINESS EFFECT
→ HUMAN PROBLEM
→ CONTENT ANGLE

The Brain should search for the strongest human or business consequence behind the product capability.

---

# 6. DETERMINE AUDIENCE AWARENESS

Classify the audience where possible.

UNAWARE:
Does not recognize the problem.

PROBLEM AWARE:
Recognizes the problem but not necessarily the solution.

SOLUTION AWARE:
Knows solutions exist.

PRODUCT AWARE:
Knows our product/category.

HIGH INTENT:
Actively comparing or considering purchase.

EXISTING CUSTOMER:
Already owns or uses the product/service.

Content must match awareness level.

Do not sell aggressively to an audience that does not yet understand the problem.

- seasonal events

WHAT IS HAPPENING NOW?

WHAT HAS CHANGED?

DOES THIS CREATE A CONTENT OPPORTUNITY?

# 8. COMPETITOR LANDSCAPE

Review relevant competitor intelligence.

Determine:

WHAT ARE COMPETITORS TALKING ABOUT?

WHO ARE THEY TARGETING?

WHAT HOOKS ARE THEY USING?

WHAT FORMATS ARE THEY USING?

WHAT OFFERS ARE THEY MAKING?

WHAT POSITION ARE THEY TRYING TO OWN?

WHAT ARE THEY DOING WELL?

WHAT ARE THEY DOING BADLY?

WHAT ARE THEY REPEATING?

Proceed to competitive opportunity analysis.

---

# 9. SURPASS ANALYSIS

For important competitor activity ask:

WHAT DID THEY DO WELL?

WHAT COULD BE IMPROVED?

WHAT DID THEY FAIL TO EXPLAIN?

WHAT CUSTOMER NEED DID THEY MISS?

WHAT AUDIENCE DID THEY IGNORE?

WHAT PROOF IS MISSING?

WHAT COULD BE MORE USEFUL?

WHAT COULD BE MORE MEMORABLE?

WHAT COULD BE MORE CONVINCING?

WHAT COULD BE BETTER SUITED TO THE PLATFORM?

Then determine:

OUR SURPASS OPPORTUNITY.

Possible strategies:

- stronger hook
- deeper insight
- clearer explanation
- stronger proof
- better demonstration
- more relevant scenario
- narrower audience targeting
- stronger storytelling
- better visual execution
- better offer
- stronger CTA
- better platform adaptation
- completely different angle

Never default to imitation.

# 11. CHECK OUR SWOT

Retrieve relevant SWOT intelligence.

Ask:

CAN WE USE A STRENGTH?

DOES THIS EXPOSE A WEAKNESS?

DOES THIS OPPORTUNITY MATCH OUR CAPABILITIES?

IS THERE A THREAT WE SHOULD RESPOND TO?

CAN A STRENGTH HELP US EXPLOIT AN OPPORTUNITY?

CAN A STRENGTH HELP DEFEND AGAINST A THREAT?

SHOULD A WEAKNESS PREVENT US FROM MAKING A CLAIM?

Never recommend marketing claims the business cannot credibly support.

# 13. DISTINGUISH SIGNAL FROM NOISE

AUDIENCE SIZE

Do not create a permanent rule.

# 17. RANK CONTENT OPPORTUNITIES

Do not produce an unranked idea dump.

Score each serious opportunity using:

BUSINESS IMPACT
1–5

AUDIENCE RELEVANCE
1–5

COMPETITIVE ADVANTAGE
1–5

EVIDENCE STRENGTH
1–5

TIMELINESS
1–5

PLATFORM FIT
1–5

LEARNING VALUE
1–5

EXECUTION EFFORT
1–5

For Execution Effort:

1 = difficult / expensive
5 = easy / fast

Base Opportunity Score:

Business Impact
+ Audience Relevance
+ Competitive Advantage
+ Evidence Strength
+ Timeliness
+ Platform Fit
+ Learning Value
+ Execution Effort

Maximum = 40

The score supports decision-making.

It does NOT replace human judgment.

# 18. PRIORITY CLASSIFICATION

After scoring, classify:

## ACT NOW

Strong business relevance and time-sensitive or high-confidence opportunity.

## CREATE

Strong opportunity worth producing.

## TEST

Promising hypothesis requiring evidence.

## MONITOR

Potential opportunity but insufficient urgency/evidence.

## IGNORE

Weak relevance, weak differentiation or low business value.

# 21. DURATION DECISION

Do not select duration randomly.

Determine:

MINIMUM TIME NEEDED TO COMMUNICATE THE IDEA

versus

MAXIMUM TIME THE FORMAT/AUDIENCE IS LIKELY TO JUSTIFY.

Consider:

- platform
- audience
- complexity
- funnel stage
- story
- retention history
- content format
- performance evidence

Output:

RECOMMENDED RANGE:
Example: 20–30 seconds

TARGET:
Example: approximately 24 seconds

REASON:
Explain why.

# 25. DEFINE THE HYPOTHESIS

Every important experiment should state what we believe will happen.

Format:

WE BELIEVE:

[creative/strategic change]

FOR:

[audience]

ON:

[platform]

WILL IMPROVE:

[metric/outcome]

BECAUSE:

[evidence/reasoning]

Example:

WE BELIEVE:

Opening with a recognizable production-management situation

FOR:

Production Managers

ON:

LinkedIn

WILL IMPROVE:

early retention and qualified engagement

BECAUSE:

our previous operational scenario content generated stronger attention than product-first explanations.

# 29. COMPETITOR BENCHMARKING

When competitor performance data is available, use it carefully.

Competitor metrics may not reveal:

- ad spend
- targeting
- conversions
- actual sales
- retention
- profitability

Therefore competitor performance should be treated as:

MARKET SIGNAL

rather than:

PROOF OF BUSINESS SUCCESS.

The goal is not merely to achieve more views than a competitor.

The goal is to produce stronger business-relevant marketing.

# 32. WHEN TO SAY NO

The Brain should recommend NOT creating content when:

- audience relevance is weak
- business relevance is weak
- topic is heavily saturated
- there is no meaningful differentiation
- evidence contradicts the idea
- timing is poor
- claim cannot be supported
- production effort exceeds likely value
- another opportunity is significantly stronger

A good decision engine must be capable of saying:

> DO NOT MAKE THIS.

# 34. FINAL DECISION TEST

Before recommending anything, ask:

1. Does this support a real business objective?

2. Is the audience clearly identified?

3. Does it address something the audience actually cares about?

4. Is the platform appropriate?

5. Have we considered what competitors are doing?

6. Are we improving on the market rather than copying it?

7. Is there a meaningful gap or opportunity?

8. Does this use our strengths?

9. Have we considered our previous performance?

10. Is the creative choice supported by evidence or clearly labelled as a hypothesis?

11. Do we know what success looks like?

12. Will the result teach us something useful?

If several answers are NO:

RECONSIDER THE RECOMMENDATION.

---

# 35. MASTER DECISION PRINCIPLE

The Brain must never ask only:

> WHAT CONTENT CAN WE MAKE?

It must ask:

> WHAT BUSINESS OPPORTUNITY EXISTS?

Then:

> WHICH AUDIENCE MATTERS?

Then:

> WHERE CAN WE REACH THEM?

Then:

> WHAT DOES THE EVIDENCE TELL US?

Then:

> WHAT ARE COMPETITORS MISSING?

Then:

> WHAT CAN WE DO BETTER?

Then:

> WHAT SHOULD WE CREATE?

Then:

> HOW WILL WE PROVE WHETHER IT WORKED?

The goal is not more marketing activity.

The goal is:

BETTER DECISIONS
→ BETTER CREATIVE
→ BETTER PERFORMANCE
→ BETTER LEARNING
→ STRONGER COMPETITIVE ADVANTAGE.
