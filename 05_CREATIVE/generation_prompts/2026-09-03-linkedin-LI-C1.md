## Concept
Six-panel carousel: **"A shift handover, panel by panel."** Walks through what actually
happens to production information at the moment one shift ends and the next begins on a
paper-run floor, then contrasts it with a single digital job record. One idea per panel,
plain language, no product screenshots (a representative labelled mock only if any UI is
shown).

## Panel-by-panel

**Panel 1 — Hook**
On-screen text: "6:59 p.m. The shift is ending. Where does everything you know about this job go?"
Visual: a wall clock at 6:59, a half-finished job on a bench, a clipboard.

**Panel 2 — The verbal handover**
Text: "Some of it is said out loud — to whoever is standing there."
Visual: two operators talking at a machine, one already walking away.

**Panel 3 — The paper note**
Text: "Some of it is written on the traveller — if there was time."
Visual: a paper job traveller with a couple of hand-written lines, a blank box.

**Panel 4 — The gap**
Text: "The rest leaves with the person who knew it."
Visual: an empty chair, lights off over one line, the next shift looking at the same job.

**Panel 5 — What a digital job record holds instead**
Text: "A digital job record keeps what was entered against the job — who recorded it, at
which step, and when."
Visual: a clean, simple representative record card (LABELLED "illustrative") showing job,
step, operator, timestamp, quantity entered.

**Panel 6 — The point + soft CTA**
Text: "The next shift starts from what's recorded, not from what they can remember to ask."
Sub-line: "That's the problem Mysoft MES works on — production information that survives the
handover."
CTA: "Follow for more on shop-floor visibility." (No link in the post body — see LinkedIn
2026 algo note; if a link is used, it goes in the first comment.)

## Tone and art direction
Documentary, plain, Malaysian SME factory — not glossy corporate 3D. Consistent restrained
palette across all six panels, one accent colour, large legible type (readable at LinkedIn
feed thumbnail size). Real-looking floor, real-looking paper. No stock-photo handshake
imagery.

## What must NOT appear
- No percentage figures, no "reduce X by Y%", no time-saved claims, no ROI, no "eliminates
  human error", no "guarantees accurate data", no implementation duration
  (`01_BUSINESS/products.md` §20, §31).
- Do not depict or describe the Digiwin AIoT upload mechanism — it is TO VERIFY
  (`products.md` §7).
- Do not claim regulatory / audit compliance outcomes (`products.md` §19, §31).
- Traceability language capped at "what the record captured" — who/what/when as *entered*,
  not "full traceability" or "proof".
- Panel 5's record card is explicitly labelled illustrative; it must not be presented as an
  actual Mysoft MES screenshot until a real capture is supplied.
- No competitor names.

## CTA
Primary: "Follow for more on shop-floor visibility." Secondary (comment, optional): link to

## Hypothesis
WE BELIEVE a 6-panel carousel telling the shift-handover story FOR production managers ON
LinkedIn WILL produce higher average dwell time and saves per impression than an equivalent
text post BECAUSE LinkedIn's 2026 ranking favours dwell time, carousels reportedly earn
2–3× the dwell of text, and no local MES competitor is using the format — so the same idea
should travel further in this format than as text.

## Success metric
- **Primary:** average dwell time per impression (or, if unavailable, saves per impression)
  vs the median of the September LinkedIn text posts.
- **Secondary:** follows generated; qualified comments (people describing their own
  handover problem); profile visits.
- **Comparison baseline:** the September LinkedIn text-post cycle once it has run. Until
  then there is no Mysoft baseline — do not set a numeric target (`decision_framework.md` §26).
