# 2. POSITIONING PRINCIPLE

Mysoft MES should not position itself simply as:

MES SOFTWARE.

It should position around the manufacturing problem it helps solve.

Core problem:

MANAGEMENT CANNOT ALWAYS CLEARLY SEE OR VERIFY WHAT IS ACTUALLY HAPPENING ON THE SHOP FLOOR.

This may result from:

- paper records
- manual Job Orders
- delayed updates
- verbal reporting
- Excel
- fragmented systems
- human recording errors
- inaccurate reports
- difficult traceability
- disconnected production information

The stronger positioning is therefore:

HELP MANUFACTURERS TURN SHOP-FLOOR ACTIVITY INTO MORE VISIBLE, STRUCTURED AND TRACEABLE PRODUCTION INFORMATION.

---

# 3. CORE CUSTOMER REALITY

Many manufacturers already have some form of:

- ERP
- production planning
- paper Job Travellers
- Excel
- whiteboards
- manual reports
- WhatsApp
- supervisors
- machine systems
- internal workflows

The problem is not necessarily:

"THE FACTORY HAS NO SYSTEM."

The problem may be:

THE EXISTING SYSTEMS DO NOT GIVE MANAGEMENT ENOUGH RELIABLE VISIBILITY INTO WHAT IS ACTUALLY HAPPENING DURING PRODUCTION.

The Brain should therefore avoid assuming every customer needs complete digital transformation from zero.

---

# 4. CORE POSITIONING

Recommended strategic positioning:

MYSOFT MES HELPS MANUFACTURERS DIGITALLY CAPTURE, TRACK AND CONNECT SHOP-FLOOR PRODUCTION INFORMATION SO MANAGEMENT CAN SEE PRODUCTION MORE CLEARLY, TRACE WHAT HAPPENED AND MAKE DECISIONS USING MORE RELIABLE DATA.

This is a strategic positioning statement.

Exact wording used publicly may vary by audience and platform.

# 5. SHORT POSITIONING

FROM MANUAL PRODUCTION RECORDS TO VISIBLE, TRACEABLE PRODUCTION DATA.

Alternative:

1. THE CUSTOMER CARES ABOUT IT

3. IT GIVES THE CUSTOMER A REASON TO CHOOSE US

Examples of weak differentiation:

- powerful
- smart
- innovative
- real-time
- all-in-one
- digital transformation

unless supported with concrete meaning and evidence.

# 15. POSITIONING AGAINST ERP

Do not position:

MES REPLACES ERP.

A safer strategic distinction is:

ERP often helps manage planning and business transactions.

MES focuses more closely on production execution and shop-floor information.

The positioning question is:

WHAT HAPPENS BETWEEN THE PRODUCTION PLAN AND THE ACTUAL WORK DONE ON THE SHOP FLOOR?

Exact boundaries depend on the customer's ERP and manufacturing environment.

---

# 16. POSITIONING AGAINST PAPER AND EXCEL

Paper and Excel are not always "bad."

They are often:

- familiar
- flexible
- inexpensive
- easy to start

Their limitations may appear as production complexity increases.

Potential limitations:

- difficult real-time visibility
- fragmented information
- manual consolidation
- missing records
- repeated entry
- traceability difficulty
- dependence on people for updates
- difficult verification

The Brain should acknowledge why customers still use them.

This creates more credible positioning.

# 19. POSITIONING AROUND FALSE OR INACCURATE REPORTING

Production information may sometimes differ from actual activity due to:

- human error
- late recording
- incorrect recording
- incomplete recording
- misunderstanding
- manual report preparation
- inaccurate reporting
- false claims

This is a real customer problem.

However, messaging must not accuse factory staff generally of dishonesty.

Safer positioning:

CAN YOU VERIFY WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED?

or:

HOW MUCH OF YOUR PRODUCTION REPORT STILL DEPENDS ON MANUAL INPUT?

or:

WHEN THE REPORT AND SHOP FLOOR DO NOT MATCH, WHICH ONE DO YOU TRUST?

The objective is:

VERIFIABILITY AND ACCOUNTABILITY.

Not:

ACCUSATION.

# 28. MARKET POSITIONING TEST

Before accepting a positioning statement, ask:

## RELEVANT

Does the customer actually care?

## DIFFERENT

Does this sound meaningfully different from competitors?

## CREDIBLE

Can Mysoft prove or deliver it?

## CLEAR

Can the audience understand it quickly?

## SPECIFIC

Does it describe a real manufacturing problem or value?

## FLEXIBLE

Can it work across relevant industries without becoming meaningless?

If the answer is weak:

REWORK THE POSITIONING.
