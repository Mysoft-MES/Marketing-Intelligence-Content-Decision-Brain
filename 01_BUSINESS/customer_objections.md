# 1. PURPOSE

This file records the objections, concerns, doubts, resistance and buying friction that may prevent a manufacturer from adopting Mysoft MES.

The Brain must use this file to understand:

- why prospects hesitate
- why prospects delay
- why prospects reject
- what they misunderstand
- what they fear
- what evidence they need
- what content should answer objections
- what sales should clarify
- what product gaps may be causing objections
- which objections indicate poor fit
- which objections indicate poor communication
- which objections indicate genuine product limitations

This file must evolve from real:

- sales conversations
- demos
- WhatsApp discussions
- customer meetings
- lost deals
- implementation discussions
- support feedback
- factory visits

Do not invent objections and treat them as customer facts.

If an objection has not yet been observed:

HYPOTHESIS.

# 2. OBJECTION PRINCIPLE

An objection is not automatically:

A REJECTION.

It may mean:

- customer does not understand
- customer does not see enough value
- risk feels too high
- timing is wrong
- budget is unavailable
- authority is missing
- implementation seems difficult
- internal politics exist
- existing process still feels acceptable
- competitor is preferred
- product capability does not fit
- evidence is insufficient

The Brain must identify:

WHAT IS THE REAL REASON BEHIND THE OBJECTION?

---

# 3. OBJECTION CLASSIFICATION

Each objection should eventually be classified as one or more of:

## VALUE OBJECTION

Customer does not believe the benefit justifies the investment.

## PRICE OBJECTION

Customer believes the cost is too high.

## PRIORITY OBJECTION

Customer believes another issue is more urgent.

## TIMING OBJECTION

Customer may be interested but not ready now.

## CHANGE OBJECTION

Customer is resistant to changing the current workflow.

## USER ADOPTION OBJECTION

Customer fears employees will not use the system.

## TECHNICAL OBJECTION

Customer questions integration, infrastructure, hardware, system compatibility or technical capability.

## IMPLEMENTATION OBJECTION

Customer fears implementation effort, disruption or complexity.

## MANAGEMENT OBJECTION

Decision-maker does not support the project.

## OPERATIONAL OBJECTION

Customer worries the system will interfere with production.

## TRUST OBJECTION

Customer lacks confidence in the vendor, technology or promised result.

## COMPETITOR OBJECTION

Customer prefers another provider.

## STATUS-QUO OBJECTION

Customer believes current process is sufficient.

## PRODUCT-FIT OBJECTION

The current solution may genuinely not fit the customer's needs.

# 4. CORE OBJECTION — "WE ALREADY HAVE ERP"

Potential customer statement:

"WE ALREADY HAVE ERP."

Possible underlying meaning:

- customer assumes ERP and MES are the same
- customer believes existing software should already solve the problem
- customer does not see the shop-floor execution gap
- customer wants to avoid another system
- customer fears integration complexity
- customer sees no additional value

Recommended reasoning:

Do not attack ERP.

Clarify the difference between:

WHAT IS PLANNED / TRANSACTED

and

WHAT IS ACTUALLY HAPPENING ON THE SHOP FLOOR.

Potential response direction:

ERP may manage planning, orders and business transactions.

The question is:

HOW ARE YOU CURRENTLY CAPTURING AND VERIFYING WHAT HAPPENS DURING PRODUCTION?

Evidence needed:

- customer's existing ERP workflow
- current shop-floor recording method
- current integration capability
- actual MES capability

Never claim that ERP cannot perform a function without checking the customer's ERP.

# 5. CORE OBJECTION — "WE ALREADY USE EXCEL"

Potential statement:

"EXCEL IS ENOUGH."

Possible underlying meaning:

- Excel is familiar
- Excel is cheap
- workflow is simple
- customer does not yet feel the pain
- customer sees no urgent reason to change
- Excel has been heavily customised internally

Do not position Excel as useless.

Ask:

- How many people update it?
- How quickly is the information updated?
- Can management see current production status?
- Can production history be traced easily?
- How much manual consolidation is required?
- What happens when records are incorrect or missing?
- How do you verify what actually happened?

MES becomes more relevant when complexity, volume, traceability or reporting requirements exceed what the current process handles comfortably.

# 6. CORE OBJECTION — "PAPER WORKS FINE"

Possible meanings:

- process is simple
- staff are comfortable with paper
- customer sees no financial pain
- customer fears digitalisation
- production environment is not ready
- customer does not trust digital systems

Do not say:

"PAPER IS BAD."

Instead identify the point where paper creates friction.

Potential questions:

- How do you locate a job?
- What happens if the Job Traveller is lost?
- How quickly can management know the current status?
- How do you consolidate information?
- How do you trace historical records?
- How do you verify corrections?
- How many times is the same information rewritten?

Positioning direction:

The issue is not paper itself.

The issue is whether paper still provides the visibility, traceability and reliability the operation requires.

# 10. CORE OBJECTION — "MY WORKERS WILL NOT USE IT"

Possible underlying concerns:

- older workforce
- low digital literacy
- resistance to change
- fear of increased workload
- operators do not see personal benefit
- management has failed with previous systems
- interface seems complicated

This is often an implementation and change-management issue.

Questions:

- Who needs to use the system?
- What exactly must they input?
- How many steps are required?
- What current work does the system replace?
- Is training required?
- Is the workflow more difficult than paper?
- Are operators being asked to duplicate data?

Potential principle:

A production system that creates significantly more shop-floor work may fail even if management likes the dashboard.

Product and implementation teams should learn from repeated adoption objections.

# 22. CORE OBJECTION — "CAN IT PREVENT FALSE REPORTING?"

This is a sensitive but important objection.

Correct reasoning:

A digital system can create:

- timestamps
- user records
- production history
- structured data
- traceability
- stronger verification mechanisms

depending on capability.

But it cannot automatically guarantee that all entered information is true.

Do not promise:

"NO MORE FALSE REPORTING."

Safer response direction:

The system can help make production activity more traceable and easier to verify, depending on how data is captured.

# 53. OBJECTION RESPONSE RULE

Never answer an objection only to "win the argument."

The goal is to determine:

IS THE CUSTOMER'S CONCERN VALID?

If yes:

address it honestly.

If unknown:

verify.

If it reveals a product weakness:

record it.

If the customer is a poor fit:

do not force the sale.

Credibility is more valuable than winning every objection.

- customer priorities

- product weakness

- communication weakness

- buying process
- product opportunity
