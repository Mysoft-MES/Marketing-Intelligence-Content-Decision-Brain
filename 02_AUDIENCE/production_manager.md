# Production Manager

## Profile

_(Evidence-backed profile — first pass, external/industry research. Not yet validated against Mysoft's own sales or customer data. This is Mysoft's core problem-owner audience per `01_BUSINESS/company_profile.md` §10-19. Last updated: 2026-09-02.)_

- **Role and responsibilities:** Production execution, job/work-order status, manpower, shop-floor coordination, production reporting. VERIFIED FACT (internal business knowledge, `01_BUSINESS/company_profile.md` §10-19), HIGH confidence.
- **KPIs:** On-time job completion, output vs. plan, downtime, scrap/rework rate. INDUSTRY SOURCE — general manufacturing-reporting literature (Jodoo Blog, "Production Reporting Software," accessed 2026-09-02; Harmoni, "Manufacturing Production Reports," accessed 2026-09-02), not Malaysia-specific. LOW-MEDIUM confidence.
- **Daily frustrations:** Chasing down where a job is; operators recording output on paper that office staff must re-key at end of shift, creating a gap between real machine/floor state and what management sees. INDUSTRY SOURCE — Zoomo Tech (Malaysian custom-software/MES vendor, tracked competitor `04_COMPETITORS/zoomo-tech.md`), "Why Your Factory Production Status is Always Delayed," accessed 2026-09-02: describes Malaysian SME factories specifically. MEDIUM confidence (Malaysia-specific but vendor-authored, competitive source — treat per `00_SYSTEM/evidence_rules.md` §22-23 as evidence of what a competitor claims/observes, not independently verified fact).
- **Operational problems:** In electronics-assembly-style environments, a yield drop can go unnoticed for hours because reporting only surfaces at end-of-shift summary. INDUSTRY SOURCE — Jodoo Blog, accessed 2026-09-02. LOW confidence (illustrative/anecdotal example, not Malaysia-specific, not a measured statistic).
- **Business risks:** Manual entry error rates make shift data unreliable for scheduling decisions; over 70% of Malaysian SME production issues attributed to missing information rather than lack of operator skill. INDUSTRY SOURCE — Smart4wrd, accessed 2026-09-02 (Malaysia-specific). MEDIUM confidence (single vendor source; figure not independently verified).
- **Emotional pressures:** Held accountable for output and delays that may stem from information gaps rather than execution failures — "operators relying on memory risk missing critical steps, supervisors find out about problems after delays or errors have already happened" (Smart4wrd). MEDIUM confidence, same source caveats as above.
- **Questions and objections:** Concern that a new system disrupts the existing floor workflow rather than fitting into it. INDUSTRY SOURCE — Zoomo Tech, accessed 2026-09-02: "A well-designed Production Tracking System for Malaysian SME fits into existing workflows, without disrupting daily work on the floor." MEDIUM confidence (competitor/vendor framing — useful as a signal of what this audience is sensitive to, not proof Mysoft addresses it better).
- **Purchase motivations and triggers:** Desire for simple, low-friction data capture at the source (e.g. basic "start/stop" input) rather than a full ERP overhaul; desire to catch problems (bottlenecks, yield drops) while they are still happening, not after the shift ends. INDUSTRY SOURCE — Zoomo Tech and Harmoni ("How to Improve Real-Time Production Visibility," accessed 2026-09-02). MEDIUM confidence.
- **Rejection triggers:** Fear of added administrative burden, fear of workflow disruption, distrust of systems that don't match how the floor actually operates.
- **Information needed before buying:** How the system captures data without adding operator workload; how it integrates with existing paper/Excel-based routines during transition.
- **Preferred content and platforms:** Not yet role-specific in existing platform research — see `02_AUDIENCE/audience_matrix.md` for company-wide channel/format findings (LinkedIn for decision-influencer content, Facebook for owner/GM-level decision-makers).
- **Content angles and hooks:** Operational-pain-first scenarios (e.g. "boss asks where a job is") are indicated as a stronger direction than product-first messaging per existing `05_CREATIVE` hook research — see `05_CREATIVE/hook_library.md`.
- **Language used / messages to avoid:** Avoid abstract "digital transformation" language; favour concrete shop-floor scenarios (job status, operator reporting, shift handover).
- **Funnel considerations:** Likely PROBLEM AWARE — lives the paper/manual-reporting pain daily but may not initiate the purchase decision (per `01_BUSINESS/company_profile.md` §18-19, Production Manager is typically a Problem Owner/User, not necessarily Budget Owner).
- **Evidence and customer observations:** UNKNOWN — no direct Mysoft production-manager customer interviews on file yet. Evidence gap: the 70%-missing-information figure and the "yield drop unnoticed for hours" example both come from vendor-authored sources (one Malaysian, one not) and should be treated as hypotheses to test against Mysoft's own prospect/customer conversations, not settled facts.
